Wildfire Impacts and Fire Danger

Fire progression, fire danger, climate drivers, and ecosystem response

We study wildfire impacts and fire danger as coupled climate, vegetation, and ecosystem processes. Current work includes satellite-based analysis of wildfire progression, future fire danger under changing climate, and the ecological consequences of fire across natural resource systems.

Core Questions

  • How do climate, weather, vegetation, and terrain interact to control wildfire progression?
  • How will fire danger change across the Southern Great Plains and other regions under future climate scenarios?
  • How do wildfire disturbance and vegetation change affect ecosystem carbon, water, and resilience?

Methods

  • Process-based ecosystem and fire-relevant modeling.
  • Satellite remote sensing of fire progression, fuels, soil moisture, and landscape condition.
  • Climate projection analysis and fire danger diagnostics.
  • Geospatial workflows for regional and continental-scale analysis.

Outputs

  • Peer-reviewed wildfire and fire danger studies.
  • Spatial datasets and maps for fire-climate analysis.
  • Public fire danger data products and reproducible workflows.
