Water Resources and Biogeochemical Cycles

Carbon-water exchanges, evapotranspiration, ecohydrology, and resource availability

We study ecosystem biogeochemical cycles and water resources availability, with emphasis on how land cover, vegetation change, climate, and management affect evapotranspiration, carbon exchange, soil moisture, and hydrologic services.

Core Questions

  • How do forests, croplands, grasslands, and woody encroachment differ in water use and carbon exchange?
  • How will irrigation water requirements change across U.S. croplands under future climate?
  • How do ecosystem services related to water storage, water supply, and climate regulation interact?

Methods

  • Process-based ecosystem and hydrologic modeling.
  • Remote sensing of evapotranspiration, soil moisture, vegetation, and land cover.
  • Ecohydrologic analysis of water storage and water availability.
  • Carbon-water flux analysis using observations and model simulations.

Outputs

  • Studies of water resource availability under climate change.
  • Analyses of carbon and water trade-offs in changing ecosystems.
  • Spatial datasets supporting hydrology, irrigation, and ecosystem service research.
