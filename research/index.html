Research

Our group studies wildfire as a coupled climate, vegetation, weather, and human-system problem. We combine physical modeling, remote sensing, geospatial analysis, and operational data workflows to support scientific understanding and practical risk management.

Research Themes

Fire Danger and Operational Indices

We produce and evaluate daily fire danger indices such as Burning Index, Energy Release Component, Spread Component, and related fire-weather metrics. The goal is to make model outputs easier to interpret, compare, archive, and use in preparedness planning.

Fire Weather and Climate Extremes

We investigate how heat waves, drought, wind, humidity, and atmospheric circulation influence the timing and severity of wildfire danger. This work links daily meteorology with longer-term climate variability and change.

Remote Sensing of Fuels and Burned Areas

Satellite observations help us characterize vegetation condition, fuel moisture proxies, burned area patterns, and post-fire recovery. We focus on workflows that can be reproduced across regions and updated as new imagery becomes available.

Decision Support and Open Data

We build transparent tools for sharing fire risk information with researchers, land managers, and partner agencies. Our products emphasize metadata, versioned processing, and clear uncertainty communication.
